# 0077. Theme families as variants and tokens

- **Status:** accepted (M45, 2026-10-17; D32 decided 2026-10-16), amended 2026-10-19 ([Classic is the default and listed first](#amendment-2026-10-19-classic-is-the-default-and-listed-first), D36) and 2026-10-22 ([Classic's editor colors follow the classic IDE](#amendment-2026-10-22-classics-editor-colors-follow-the-classic-ide), D47; [Classic's rounded corners](#amendment-2026-10-22-classics-rounded-corners), D46)
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Deciders:** maintainer
- **Amends:** [ADR 0019](0019-classic-ide-look-and-feel.md) (themes as tokens: Blue, Light, Dark and High Contrast become the Classic family)

## Context

The maintainer asked for a look "close to the classic IDE's style but original too" and chose all four directions of the design ([`design/shell-themes.md`](../design/shell-themes.md)): Harbor, Graphite, Daylight and Ember, each Light, Dark and High contrast, with every color in the design's tables. D32 settled the rest: Harbor following the system for new installs, today's four themes kept as a Classic family so a saved setting keeps its look, and `workbench.colorTheme` taking `<family>` (follows the system) or `<family>.<light|dark|highContrast>`. The roadmap asked for an ADR, before code, on how a family's shapes become tokens and style classes so that views don't change. What had to be decided:
- how twelve more themes sit next to Classic's four, with Fluent's controls, Dock's templates, the editor and the terminal following;
- what the setting's values are, how today's are read, and what a new install gets;
- how the families' layout differences (gutters, corners, tabs, captions, the status bar) are expressed without per-family views;
- how a plugin's color theme keeps applying.

## Decisions

**Each family variant is an Avalonia theme variant.** `ThemeService` defines twelve `ThemeVariant`s (`Harbor.Light`, `Harbor.Dark`, `Harbor.HighContrast`…), each inheriting Classic's variant of its kind: Light, Dark, or Classic's High Contrast (itself inheriting Dark). Fluent's palettes only know Light and Dark, so the inheritance gives every family variant Fluent's colors of its kind, and High contrast variants Classic's high-contrast Fluent overrides (black menus, outlined buttons). Each variant's dictionary sits in `Theming/Tokens.axaml`'s `ThemeDictionaries`, next to Classic's: Avalonia searches a dictionary's own theme dictionaries, with inheritance, before its merged ones, so a family in a merged file would lose to Classic's Light or Dark it inherits. Each family dictionary sets every `Shell.*` token itself (a test reads the file and checks it), so nothing of Classic's leaks through the inheritance; Dock's brushes; and the Fluent keys where the family's colors matter: text boxes and combo boxes (`Shell.Input`), list and tree selection (`Shell.Selection`, `Shell.Selection.Foreground`), check boxes, toggle switches and accent buttons (the accent), and in High contrast white borders and the accent as focus.

**The design is the source of the colors.** The design's tables are copied as they are. A token the design leaves out takes the family's panel colors (the design's rule: captions, Daylight's editor background, Ember's text boxes), and the others (hover, overlays, the status bar's hover, tab states, project tab colors, inlay hints, Fluent overrides) are mixes of the family's own colors or Classic's state colors of the same kind, chosen to meet every contrast pair. The tests read `design/shell-themes.md` and check every color of every table in every variant, and the editor's.

**New tokens**, in every theme (Classic's keep its look: its values are what it drew before):
- from the design: `Shell.Selection.Foreground`, `Shell.Input`, `Shell.Editor.CurrentLine`, `Shell.Editor.Breakpoint.Line`, `Shell.Editor.CurrentStatement`, `Shell.Editor.Breakpoint` (the debugger's adornments now draw them);
- found while applying it: `Shell.StatusBar.Empty.Foreground` and `.Debugging.Foreground` with their `.Hover` (Ember's bar is the window's color with dark text, white only while debugging; a High contrast bar without a solution is black under white text); `Shell.TitleBar.Hover` and `Shell.TitleBar.Chip` (Harbor Light's navy title bar: the menu bar's items and the solution's name on it); `Shell.Search.Foreground` (the search box on that title bar); `Shell.Toolbar.Start`, `.Foreground` and `.Icon` (Daylight's Start button filled with the accent).

**Shapes are tokens too, applied by `ShellStyles.axaml`.** A theme dictionary can hold any value, and `DynamicResource` follows the variant, so a family's shape is a resource next to its colors, and a style applies it to Dock's template parts and the shell's chrome. No view changes, no style class per family, and floating windows follow on their own:
- `Shell.Gutter` and `Shell.Gutter.Background`: the splitter between docked windows (`ProportionalStackPanelSplitter.Thickness`): Harbor's 6 px and Ember's 8 px of window, Graphite's and Daylight's 1 px hairline, Classic Dock's 4 px, transparent.
- `Shell.Panel.CornerRadius.Top` and `.Bottom`: Ember's 10 px cards (the tool window's caption and content, the document well's content, clipped).
- Dock's own `DockDocumentTabItemCornerRadius` and `DockDocumentTabItemMargin`: Daylight's and Ember's rounded tabs.
- `Shell.Tab.Active.Indicator` and `.IndicatorBrush`: the active document tab's border, on Dock's `PART_TabGutter` (every tab has it, transparent, so the active one doesn't move): Harbor's 3 px accent bar on top, Graphite's 2 px underline, Ember's 1 px edge.
- `Shell.Caption.Active.FontWeight`: the focused tool window's title, bold where the family has no caption color; `Shell.Caption.Active.Marker` and `.MarkerPadding`: Graphite's accent dot before it, a drawing brush as the title's background.
- `Shell.StatusBar.ShowDot`: Ember's state dot, an ellipse in the status bar colored by the state.

**The setting** (D32). `harbor` follows the system, `harbor.dark` doesn't; Classic is `classic`, `classic.blue`, `classic.light`, `classic.dark` and `classic.highContrast`. The values written before M45 (`followSystem`, `blue`, `light`, `dark`, `highContrast`) **read as Classic** and stay valid values of the setting, so a saved setting keeps its look and isn't reported, and the file isn't rewritten (an older version still reads it). The default is `harbor`, so an install without the setting gets Harbor following the system; M46's setup will offer the choice. (Since 2026-10-19 the default is `classic`: see the [amendment](#amendment-2026-10-19-classic-is-the-default-and-listed-first).) An unknown value resolves as the default. Following the system resolves within the family: the OS's high contrast first, then its light or dark (`PlatformSettings.ColorValuesChanged` re-applies it).

**What the rest of the shell sees.** `ThemeService.ActiveTheme` is Classic's `blue`, `light`, `dark` or `highContrast`, as before, or a family's `harbor.dark`; `ActiveFamily` its family. The `theme` context key stays the kind (`light`, `dark`, `highContrast`; Classic's `blue`), so plugins' `when` clauses keep working, and a new **`themeFamily`** key names the family. The editor takes its kind's colors with the family's C# classification colors (keywords, types, methods, strings, numbers, comments) and current line over them; TextMate grammars and the terminal's ANSI palette follow the kind, the terminal's background and text are the family's editor's. Fonts and Colors still goes over all of it; its customizations take a family's name (`"[Harbor]"`) for the three variants and the variant's (`"[Harbor Dark]"`) over it, and Classic's keep their names (`"[Dark]"`, `"[High Contrast]"`).

**Plugins.** A contributed color theme applies over Classic's variant for its `uiTheme` base, as it did (M22a); `themeFamily` is `classic` then. The plugin API doesn't change.

**Choosing.** Color Theme (the quick pick) lists each family following the system and in each variant, Classic's, then the plugins'; moving previews (`ThemeService.Preview`) and Enter saves. Tools → Options → Environment → Appearance shows the setting as the families' cards, each with a picture drawn from that family's tokens in the chosen mode (read by its view model: a `ThemeVariantScope` drew nothing when its variant was the window's), a mode (Follow System, Light, Dark, High Contrast; Classic also Blue), and a plugin's theme from its own list.

## Consequences

- The twelve variants add about 2,100 lines of tokens to `Tokens.axaml` (compiled XAML) and no code paths per family. Startup applies one variant as before; a switch is a resource change.
- Contrast: every pair in `contrast-pairs.txt`, which gained the design's new pairs, holds in all sixteen themes; text in the families' High contrast variants is held to 7:1, the design's rule, and the design's syntax colors are checked against the editor and its current line.
- The structural differences in the design that need more than a value are left: Daylight's tool window rail and its toolbar's rounded groups, Ember's run controls in a pill in the title bar and its Locals empty state, Graphite's modified-tab dot and its search box in the middle of the title row, tool window tabs in the header (Graphite, Daylight, Ember). Each needs a template or a view of its own and its own decision. `TODO(issue)`
- The smoke test's gallery photographs the sixteen themes only with `CODALUS_GALLERY_THEMES=all` (four times the pictures, more than the smoke test's time on CI); its `shell.themeFamilies` step photographs the window in each family variant on every run. `TODO(issue)`: the full gallery on CI.

## Amendment (2026-10-19): Classic is the default and listed first

The maintainer's choice (D36), from a screenshot of the first-run setup and Options: "The classic theme should be offered in the setup wizard, be first in the list and be the default." It replaces D32's Harbor as the default; nothing else above changes.

- **The default** is `classic`: Classic following the system, within the family as every plain id does: the OS's high contrast gives Classic High Contrast (the `HighContrast` variant, icons monochrome as before), then its light or dark gives Classic Light or Dark. Following the system never picks Blue, as `followSystem` never did. An unknown value resolves as the default, so now as Classic.
- **First in every list:** `ThemeService.Families` starts with Classic, so it's the first card in Options, the first entries of the Color Theme quick pick, the first values of the setting (its description names Classic first, as the default), and the first card of the setup. VS Code's import names them in that order.
- **The setup** shows five cards, Classic first and preselected with Match system, narrower so that they fit one row at the window's size. Its mode control (Light, Dark, High contrast, Match system) applies to every card, so on Classic it picks Classic's Light, Dark and High Contrast. **Blue stays in Options** (and the quick pick): only Classic has it, and a fifth mode that four cards lack would be a control that does nothing there. When the settings say Classic Blue, the setup shows the Classic card with no mode chosen and a note that Blue is in Options, and keeps it unless a mode or another card is chosen; another card matches the system, as Options' cards do for a family without the mode.
- **Profiles without the setting move to Classic.** The settings file isn't rewritten (the rule above), so a profile without `workbench.colorTheme` shows the default, and nothing can tell a profile that saw Harbor in 0.3.0 from one of 0.2 that saw Classic without writing to it. Moving them is the sensible result: every profile from before 0.3.0 had Classic following the system (the old `followSystem` default), so they get that look back; Harbor was the default only in 0.3.0 (released 2026-10-18) and its nightlies; and it's what the maintainer chose. Who prefers Harbor picks it once (the changelog says so).
- **A setting that's there is untouched:** every saved value, `harbor` and the values from before M45 included, reads as before.
- **The setup records its choice.** Next on the Appearance step now also writes the theme when the settings have none, even when it's the default, so a choice made in the setup stays the user's if the default changes again. (0.3.0's setup didn't write a Harbor that was the default, so those profiles move to Classic with the others.) Skip setup still writes nothing.
- The plugin API, the context keys and the tokens don't change. The smoke test's gallery stays at Classic's four themes and Harbor's three; its setup picture (`setup-appearance.png`, the website's) is now Classic Light.

## Amendment (2026-10-22): Classic's editor colors follow the classic IDE

The maintainer reported Classic's colors as muted next to the classic IDE's (#83). Most of it was the document view drawn at 0.85 opacity (Dock's reveal fade, turned off in 0.6); D47 decided that the real palette differences follow the classic IDE's measured values too. The values and what was assumed are in [`design/shell-themes.md`](../design/shell-themes.md#classic-06).

- **Classic's own editor colors change, the families' don't.** `EditorTheme.Dark` and `.Light` (Classic's, which the families start from) take the classic IDE's control keywords, comments and strings, and Dark's current line `#272727`. The families set their keywords, types, methods, strings, numbers, comments and current line themselves, as above; their control keywords, which they took from Classic, are now set to the value they had (`FamilyDarkControlKeyword`, `FamilyLightControlKeyword`), so no family changes.
- **Two new tokens in every theme:** `Shell.Editor.LineNumbers.Current`, the caret line's number, drawn by `CurrentLineNumberMargin` (AvaloniaEdit's line number margin, replaced in the editor's margins: AvaloniaEdit's draws one brush), and `Shell.Editor.CodeLens.Foreground`, CodeLens's text (it shared the inlay hints' color). The families and High Contrast set them to their line numbers' and inlay hints' colors. Themes and `workbench.colorCustomizations` reach them as `editorLineNumber.activeForeground` and `editorCodeLens.foreground`.
- **A setting's default can follow the family.** Bracket pair colors are off in the classic IDE, so `editor.bracketPairColorization` defaults to `false` in Classic and `true` in the families: the shell registers the setting's descriptor again with the active family's default when the family changes. A value in `settings.json` wins either way, and the Settings UI shows the default in effect. This is the settings system's own mechanism (a registration replaces the descriptor and raises `Changed` for the keys whose value changed), not a special case in the reader.
- **TextMate's fallback follows** in Classic: Dark+'s and Light+'s control keyword, comment and string colors are replaced with Classic's (`TextMateSupport.RecolorsFor`), as Dark+'s plain text already was.

## Amendment (2026-10-22): Classic's rounded corners

D46 (#84) gives Classic the classic IDE's current shapes, as drawn in [`design/controls/RoundedCorners.dc.html`](../design/controls/RoundedCorners.dc.html); it supersedes D33's 2 px Classic corners. The shapes stay tokens applied by `ShellStyles.axaml`, as above, with three additions:
- **One control theme.** The document well is one card holding its tab strip: a `DocumentDockControl` control theme in `ShellStyles.axaml`'s resources wraps Dock's 15-line template (Dock.Avalonia.Themes.Fluent 12.1.0.7, MIT) in a `Border` with `Shell.Card.*`. The families' card has no radius, outline or fill, so their well looks as before. The strip's own fill is transparent in Classic and the card is filled with it, so the card's corners round the strip; the card doesn't clip, because Avalonia's `Border` clips its child inside its border and the document's frame paints over the card's sides.
- **One control.** `TabStripEdge`, set as `DocumentTabStrip.BottomContent` by a style, draws the active document frame's top edge beside the selected tab, its sides and fill through that row: Dock's `DocumentControl` puts a whole border under the strip, so styles alone could only close the frame under the tab or drop its top edge. A theme turns it off with a height of 0 (every family). The tab's curved feet, the design's optional part, aren't drawn yet (`TODO(issue)`).
- **The families' values are explicit.** A family variant inherits Classic's dictionary of its kind, so every new token, and Dock's keys Classic now sets (`DockDocumentTabStripSeparatorSize`, `DockDocumentContentBorderThickness`, `DockDocumentContentBorderBrush`, Fluent's `ControlCornerRadius`), is set in each family dictionary to the value it had. A test compares the window's visual tree in every family before and after (done once while building it: only Fluent's `OverlayCornerRadius` changed, the shared popup radius), and `RoundedCornersTests` checks the families' tokens.

Every family takes the shared popup radii: `Shell.CornerRadius.Overlay` 6 (Fluent's `OverlayCornerRadius` set to it), a new `Shell.CornerRadius.Dialog` 8, and the control radius for tooltips and the editor's popups. A popup window's `TransparencyBackgroundFallback` is the window's color, so where the window system can't make its corners transparent (X11 without a compositor) they don't show white or black squares; the smoke step `shell.roundedCorners` checks it.
